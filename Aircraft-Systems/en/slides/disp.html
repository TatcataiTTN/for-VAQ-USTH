Electronic displays

A Vietnamese and English transcript of each slide, read directly from the image (36/36 slides). Module using this deck: M06. Where a slide prints something wrong or inconsistent it is listed under "Slide errors"; the transcript keeps what the slide prints.

Slide 1: Analog and digital system: Electronics Displays

Title slide "Analog and digital system / Electronics Displays": a real PFD photo and a block diagram of avionics (MCP, EFCP, PFD1/2, MFD1/2, EWD, ADS, AHRS, GPS, ATR). Footer: "Course based on THALES and Aerogates documentation".

Slide 2: Aircraft Display System (PFD, ND, EWD/SD, MFD)

1 Performance requirements: operational (aircraft environment, wide temperature range, vibration, shock, humidity, lifetime), optical (high brightness and contrast, accurate colour, wide viewing angle, readable in sun and at night), physical (size, weight, power, heat, standard form factor such as 6 x 8 inches). 2 Human factors: task and HMI. Services and system requirements: safety (BITE, redundancy, no misleading information), certification (DO-160 environment, DO-178 software, DO-254 hardware, ARP 4754A / ARP 4761), functions (PFD, ND, EWD/SD, MFD, alerts), manufacturing and tools (LRU, maintenance).

Slide 3: Display system: functional approach

Five blocks: data sources (sensors, processing, broadcast, patterns generation, application), graphic generation (graphic generator with ARINC 429 / AFDX in and LVDS / DisplayPort video out), display unit, HMI (command and control) and environment (sun, temperature, vibration, humidity).

Slide 4: Non-emissive technology: LCD, an electronic light switch

Exploded LCD: backlight, rear vertical polariser, TFT glass, liquid crystal, RGB colour filter, front horizontal polariser; pixel OFF and ON; a pixel is three sub-pixels R, G, B; the TFT is a switch (gate line selects the row, data line carries video, the capacitor C_LC holds the voltage until the next refresh); brightness follows a voltage of 0 to 5 V; backlight (LED, light guide, prism, diffuser). Three alignment types: TN (cheap, narrow viewing angle, early aircraft), IPS (wide angle, good colour, current leader), MVA (high contrast, deep black, high-end cockpits).

Numbers: voltage 0 to 5 V

Slide 5: Resolution

Image size = width x height in pixels; a pixel consists of R, G, B. HD 1280x720 (0.9 MP), Full HD 1920x1080 (2.1 MP), 4K UHD 3840x2160 (8.3 MP). Resolution is pixels per inch (PPI) or pixel size (mm). To make pixels invisible, the angle of one pixel must be below the acuity of the eye (about 1 arcminute = 1/60 degree = 2.91e-4 rad). Example D = 5 m: h = D x theta = 5 x 2.91e-4 = 1.45e-3 m, about 1.45 mm.

Formulas: h = D x theta

Numbers: 1.45 mm at 5 m

Checked by computation: 2.91e-4 x 5 m = 1.45 mm; 1280x720 = 0.92 MP, 1920x1080 = 2.07 MP, 3840x2160 = 8.29 MP.

Slide 6: Visual acuity and the eye

1 arcmin = 1/60 degree = 0.0167 degree; tan(1/120 degree) = (h/2)/5 m, so h = 2 x 5 x tan(1/120 deg) about 1.45 mm. Anatomy of the eye (cornea, pupil, iris, lens, vitreous, retina: rods for dim colourless light, blue, green and red cones, optic nerve). Peak spectral sensitivity 555 nm; colour bands 470 nm blue, 532 nm green, 615 nm red. Table of smallest pixel by distance: 1 m 0.29 mm; 3 m 0.87 mm; 5 m 1.45 mm; 10 m 2.91 mm; 20 m 5.82 mm.

Numbers: 555 nm; 470, 532, 615 nm

Checked by computation: 2 x tan(1/120 degree) = 2.91e-4 so the values 0.29; 0.87; 1.45; 2.91; 5.82 mm agree.

Slide 7: Cockpit history

From no cockpit to the glass cockpit: 1903 Wright Flyer (no cockpit); 1918 simple cockpit (about 10 instruments); 1969 traditional cockpit (about 600 instruments, for example Boeing 707); 1970 Boeing 747; 1976 Concorde (advanced analogue); 1990s move to CRT; 2000s LCD glass (integrated A320/A330/A340); today large multifunction LCD/OLED touch displays (A350, Boeing 787). Key points: hundreds of instruments in the 1960s-1970s, analogue instruments from about 1918, then the move to digital (CRT then LCD).

Numbers: about 10 instruments (1918); about 600 instruments (1969)

Slide 8: Airbus cockpit evolution

1978 A310 (first multifunction displays; the slide writes "EICAS" for the A310), 1988 A320 (six CRT displays, electrical fly-by-wire, FMS), 2007 A380 (AMLCD, IMA, interactive devices FCU/MCDU/keypad), 2015 A350 (large high-resolution touch displays), 2030? (Odisis: one free multi-touch display). The cockpit is designed to simplify tasks, increase situational awareness, and increase safety and efficiency.

Slide errors and doubts
  • Doubt: Airbus uses ECAM, not EICAS (Boeing's system); the slide writes "EICAS" for the A310.

Slide 9: Cockpit Display System (ten blocks)

Ten blocks: operational needs; concept of cockpit (analogue to digital); human factors (viewing angle 20 to 40 degrees); architecture (inputs ADC, FMS/GPS/IRS, EICAS, TCAS, VHF/HF/datalink; PFD, ND, SD/EWD; avionics data bus ARINC 429/AFDX; DCU; display units; CDU/keyboard and touch controls); building blocks; performance (resolution, viewing angle at least 170 degrees, low latency, MTBF); cost and maintainability; airborne constraints; safety and certification (DO-178C, DO-254, DO-160); installation.

Numbers: viewing angle at least 170 degrees

Slide 10: System complexity and cognitive resources

If the cognitive resources the system demands exceed human capacity, HMI performance falls (comfort zone versus overload). Cognitive engineering principles: Routinize, Anticipate, Delegate, Schematize. Examples of overload versus balance.

Numbers: Illustrative display of the complex system: EGT 640 and 642; N2 93.5 and 93.4; FF 2500 and 2490; ALT 10000 (illustrative numbers)

Slide 11: A320 versus A350 flight deck

A320: six displays (EFIS captain 2, ECAM 2 in the middle, EFIS first officer 2). A350: large 15-inch displays plus OIS, same philosophy to ease pilot transition (cross crew qualification). Legend: PFD, ND, ECAM, MB (mode control panel / MCDU / FCU).

Numbers: A350: 15-inch screens

Slide 12: Evolution of display architecture

1 Monobloc (one box, dedicated hardware, fixed 2D graphics); 2 Segregated (back end with its own functions, A320 family); 3 Integrated modular (common computing platform running several applications, 2D/3D graphics, A350 and the newest A320).

Slide 13: EFI (Electronic Flight Indicator)

The EFI shows flight parameters on a screen in place of electromechanical instruments; widely used on trainer and military aircraft (Embraer Tucano, KAI KT-1, HAL IJT 36, MiG 21, Tornado); sizes 3 ATI or 4 ATI; LCD. Structure: sensor data (ADIRU, air data, FMS/GPS) over ARINC 429, electronic processor, LCD (LED backlight, panel, polariser, glass), flight information output.

Numbers: Sizes 3 ATI or 4 ATI; example display IAS 200, ALT 4600

Slide 14: EIS = EFIS + ECAM

The EIS comprises the EFIS (PFD, ND) and the ECAM (EWD above, SD below). EFIS takes flight, navigation and control data; ECAM takes engine, system and warning data; both receive from the system computers (ADIRU, FMGC, OMS).

Numbers: ECAM example: FOB 18600 KG; TAT +15 °C, SAT +15 °C; time 13 H 28

Slide 15: Airbus EIS: CRT family

The first EIS (A310, A300-600): 5 x 5 inch CRT, VDU generating symbols. Airbus EIS 1 (A318-A321, A330-A340): 6 x 6 inch CRT, DMC or Diehl generating the graphics.

Numbers: CRT 5 x 5 inch; 6 x 6 inch

Slide 16: EIS 2: LCDU 725 and DMC

EIS 2 uses the LCDU 725 (LCD) with the DMC (Diehl). LCDU 725 modules: housing, interconnection, power supply (115 VAC to DC), pre-processing, graphic processor, video processor (optional), LCD assembly, backlight. Signal flow: aircraft systems (ADIRU, FMGC, FADEC, OMS) over ARINC 429/AFDX to the DMC, then the LCDU.

Numbers: 115 VAC

Slide 17: Evolution F3: EDU 725 and EEIS

EDU 725 (Enhanced Display Unit) and EEIS (Enhanced Electronic Instrument System), aiming to reduce cost, weight and the number of LRUs; boards ICB (interface control), VCGP (video graphic processor), DMB (display module), BLU (backlight unit), PSB (power supply), LAM; a 6 x 6 inch CRT is still shown.

Slide 18: Flight Deck Information System A320

Layout: PFD1, ND1, ECAM (upper and lower), ND2, PFD2; DMC1/2/3, FWC1/2, SDAC1/2 on ARINC 429; ECAM control box; master warning/caution; "SDAC (System Data Acquisition Concentrator)".

Slide 19: CMC (Centralized Maintenance Computer)

LRUs send data over ARINC 429 and discrete signals; CMC1 (active), CMC2 (standby); time stamp clock; MCDU1/2, printer, ACARS (optional). Example: MCDU MENU to CFDS.

Slide 20: ACARS

Units: ACMU, VHF 3, RMP 1/2/3, MCDU 1/2/3, printer, FWC, SDAC (written "System Data Analog Computer" here), DMU, CMC, FMGC. Message types: OOOI, FMS, equipment status, ping, free-text pilot messages. Operation in four steps: collect data, build the message, send and receive over VHF (VDL Mode 2), display or print.

Slide errors and doubts
  • Internally inconsistent: slide 20 spells out SDAC as "System Data Analog Computer", slide 18 as "System Data Acquisition Concentrator".

Slide 21: A320 PFD

Annunciator window (MACH, ALT, NAV), mode information (AP1, FD, A/THR), speed scale, attitude indicator (blue sky, brown ground), altitude scale, vertical speed scale (+-6000 ft/min), heading scale (30 degrees each side).

Numbers: +-6000 ft/min; 30 degrees each side

  • Label error: the range knob in the photo carries the labels 20, 40, 80, 160, 320 and a second 80 on the left (that position should be 10 NM as on a real EFIS panel); the callout of the slide says 20 / 40 / 80 / 160 / 320 NM.

Slide 23: ND ROSE ILS and ROSE VOR

ROSE ILS mode (ILS approach, mode ILS APP): GS 138 and TAS 145 kt, wind 140°/07 kt, ILS information top right (ILS2 109.3 MHz, CRS 265° selected course, IGLC identified runway), range scale with concentric rings (typically 5 NM and 10 NM in this example), compass rose 0-360°, glide slope needle (vertical guidance, deviation up or down) and localiser needle (lateral guidance, deviation left or right); used during an ILS approach for runway landing, the aircraft symbol shows position and heading. ROSE VOR mode (VOR navigation): GS 483 and TAS 451 kt, wind 210°/45 kt, VOR 1 information (112.8 MHz, CRS 220° selected course, OOD = out of range, station not received), bearing pointer (bearing to the selected VOR station), selected course line (CRS 220° in blue), bottom left and right show VOR 1 and VOR 2 name or status and distance in NM.

Numbers: ROSE ILS: GS 138, TAS 145 kt; wind 140°/07 kt; ILS2 109.3 MHz; CRS 265°; range rings 5 NM and 10 NM; ROSE VOR: GS 483, TAS 451 kt; wind 210°/45 kt; VOR 1 112.8 MHz; CRS 220°; OOD means out of range

Slide 24: ND ROSE NAV and ARC NAV

ROSE NAV: 360 degrees with the aircraft fixed in the centre and a rotating compass. ARC NAV: forward view of about 80 degrees with the aircraft at the lower centre; weather radar, range rings 20/40 NM.

Numbers: ARC: about 80 degrees

Slide 25: ECAM E/WD (engine and warning display)

E/WD: EPR (1.0-1.6), N1 (fan), N2 (written "low pressure turbine speed"), EGT, FF, FLX/TOGA, FOB 16820 KG, messages (SEAT BELTS, NO SMOKING, GND SPLRS ARMED, T.O INHIBIT). Slide 27 repeats this content.

Numbers: E/WD example: EPR 1.498 on both engines; FF 9180 kg/h; EGT 741 °C; N1 85.1 %; N2 91.7 %; FOB 16820 KG; FLX 1.498 and 49 °C; normal EPR about 1.0 to 1.6; N1 idle about 20 to 25 %; N2 about 90 to 100 % at takeoff

Slide errors and doubts
  • Does not match Tooley: the slide says N2 is the low-pressure turbine speed, while Tooley 1.1.6 says N2 is the high-pressure spool speed and the slide itself says N1 is the low-pressure spool.

Slide 26: ECAM overview and control panel

Two display screens and a control panel in the centre of the cockpit; ECAM receives data from aircraft systems over the avionics network and processes it in the CFDIU (Centralized Fault Display Interface Unit). Upper ECAM display (engine and warning): engine parameters EPR, N1, EGT, N2, fuel on board (FOB 5920 KG) and status messages (flaps and slats, PARK BRK, TCAS STBY, APU AVAIL). Lower ECAM display (system synoptic): system schematic, real-time parameters and pump and valve status; example HYD page with 3000 psi in the green, blue and yellow systems, TAT +15 °C, SAT +15 °C, GW 59205 KG. Control panel: upper and lower display brightness knobs; system page keys (ENG, BLEED, PRESS, ELEC, HYD, FUEL, APU, COND, DOOR, WHEEL, F/CTL, ALL), T.O CONFIG, EMER CANC (cancel the current warning message), CLR (clear message), STS (status), RCL (recall), CLB (climb page).

Numbers: FOB 5920 KG; GW 59205 KG; TAT +15 °C, SAT +15 °C; HYD 3000 psi in the three systems

Slide errors and doubts
  • Internally inconsistent: this slide says ECAM is processed in the CFDIU, slide 18 says DMC, FWC and SDAC.

Slide 34: F/CTL page

Airbus A320 ECAM flight control (F/CTL) page. Left screen, normal operation (green, blue and yellow hydraulic systems pressurised, ELAC and SEC OK): the top line G B Y shows the hydraulic pressure available; the boxes ELAC 1 and 2 and SEC 1, 2 and 3 are green (computer OK); aileron, elevator and rudder computers OK. Right screen, all flight controls stopped (G B Y not energised, no hydraulic pressure, ELAC and SEC failed): the computers are shown failed (no green boxes). Symbol legend: green box = computer OK; yellow dashed box = computer failed or not available; G, B, Y = green, blue, yellow hydraulic pressure available; solid green line = control law normal; dashed line = control law not available; ELAC = elevator aileron computer; SEC = spoiler elevator computer; AIL = ailerons; ELEV = elevators; RUD = rudder; SPD BRK = spoilers / speed brake. Simplified flight control system: left system (green), centre system (yellow), right system (blue); ELAC 1 and 2 and SEC 1, 2 and 3 act on the ailerons, the spoilers (SPD BRK), the left and right elevators and the rudder. Key points: the page shows the status of the flight control computers and hydraulic availability; ELAC 1/2 control the ailerons and part of the elevator functions; SEC 1/2/3 control the spoilers and the elevator functions (backup); the elevator computers control the left and right elevators; the rudder computers control the rudder; if G B Y is not energised (no hydraulic pressure) all flight control computers become unavailable and all flight controls are stopped. Display values: pitch trim 0.0° UP, TAT +15 °C, SAT +15 °C, UTC 07 H 25, GW 69205 KG.

Numbers: Pitch trim 0.0° UP; TAT +15 °C, SAT +15 °C; UTC 07 H 25; GW 69205 KG; ELAC 1 and 2, SEC 1, 2 and 3

Slide 35: F/CTL display in left and right turns

Airbus A320 ECAM flight control (F/CTL) system display in a left turn (spoilers deployed to the left) and in a right turn (spoilers deployed to the right). Spoilers: green upward arrows mean the spoilers are deployed; in the left screen the left spoilers are deployed, so the aircraft turns left (rolls left); in the right screen the right spoilers are deployed, so it turns right. SPD BRK: speed brake position, green = retracted in this example. Pitch trim: current position, for example 0.0° UP. Ailerons (AIL): left (L) and right (R) aileron position, green = normal. Rudder (RUD): rudder position, green = centred. Control computers: ELAC 1 and 2 primary (pitch and roll), SEC 1, 2 and 3 secondary. Elevators (ELEV): left and right elevator position, green = normal. Bottom line: TAT +15 °C (total air temperature), SAT +15 °C (static air temperature), UTC time 07 H 25, GW 69205 KG (gross weight). Simplified layout of the flight control surfaces: left and right ailerons (roll control), spoilers (ground and flight), left and right elevators (pitch control), rudder (yaw control). The page is used to monitor the flight control status and detect failures (amber or red indications when abnormal).

Numbers: Pitch trim 0.0° UP; TAT +15 °C, SAT +15 °C; UTC 07 H 25; GW 69205 KG
