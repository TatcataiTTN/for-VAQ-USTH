Part 1 · Foundations

Two measures of regulation

Regulation
Line = (ΔVOUT/ΔVIN)·100 % , Load = (VNL − VFL)/VFL · 100 %
  • Line regulation: does the output stay put when the input changes
  • Load regulation: does the output stay put when the load current changes
📖 Explanation for beginners
The smaller the percentage the better.

BlockRole

📖 Explanation for beginners
Every regulator is a negative-feedback loop: sample → compare → control.
Part 1 · Foundations

The linear series regulator

Series regulator
VOUT = (1 + R2/R3)·VREF
📖 Explanation for beginners
The op-amp drives the pass transistor so the sampled voltage equals VREF.
Part 1 · Foundations

The linear shunt regulator

  • Control element in parallel with the load
  • Short-circuit current limited by R1: IL(max) = VIN/R1
📖 Explanation for beginners
Simple and self-protected against shorts, but less efficient.

Part 2 · Methods

Current limiting

Current limiting
IL(max) = 0.7 V / R4
📖 Explanation for beginners
When the drop across R4 reaches 0.7 V, Q2 conducts and steals drive from Q1.
Part 2 · Methods

The switching regulator

Step-down switching regulator
VOUT ≈ duty × VIN , duty = ton/T
📖 Explanation for beginners
The transistor is fully on or off so it dissipates little; the LC filter takes the average.
Part 2 · Methods

IC regulators: 78XX/79XX and the LM317

LM317
VOUT = 1.25 V(1 + R2/R1) + IADJ·R2
  • 78XX: fixed positive; 79XX: fixed negative
📖 Explanation for beginners
The last two digits of a 78XX/79XX give the output voltage.
Part 2 · Methods

Boosting current with an external pass transistor

External pass transistor
Rext = 0.7 V / IREG(max)
📖 Explanation for beginners
When the drop across Rext reaches 0.7 V, Qext carries the excess current.

Part 4 · Pitfalls and extensions

Heat is the main limit

Heat dissipationA linear regulator dissipates (VIN − VOUT)·IL: choose VIN just above dropout and add a heat sink.
📖 Explanation for beginners
Efficiency ≈ VOUT/VIN for a linear regulator.
Part 4 · Pitfalls and extensions

Choosing the regulator type

  • Switching is efficient but produces ripple and EMI
  • A shunt regulator always wastes current even with no load
📖 Explanation for beginners
Choose by efficiency, noise and complexity.
Part 4 · Pitfalls and extensions

Practical limits

  • VIN must exceed VOUT by the dropout voltage (≈ 2 V for 78XX, ≈ 1.5–2 V for LM317)
  • Input and output capacitors are needed to avoid oscillation
📖 Explanation for beginners
Take the dropout figure from the part’s own datasheet.

3. History and theory context

Three-terminal IC regulators (the 78XX family) made power supplies simple; switching regulators gradually replaced linear ones where efficiency matters.

4. Aircraft case study (illustrative)

Illustrative: stable supplies for avionics from a varying bus. Not a specific system.

5. Exam pitfalls

  • Mixing line and load regulation.
  • Forgetting IADJ·R2 in the LM317 formula.
  • Using VNL instead of VFL in the load-regulation denominator.
  • Confusing 78XX (positive) with 79XX (negative).
