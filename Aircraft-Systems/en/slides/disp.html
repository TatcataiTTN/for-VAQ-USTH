Slide 2: Aircraft Display System (PFD, ND, EWD/SD, MFD)

1 Performance requirements: operational (aircraft environment, wide temperature range, vibration, shock, humidity, lifetime), optical (high brightness and contrast, accurate colour, wide viewing angle, readable in sun and at night), physical (size, weight, power, heat, standard form factor such as 6 x 8 inches). 2 Human factors: task and HMI. Services and system requirements: safety (BITE, redundancy, no misleading information), certification (DO-160 environment, DO-178 software, DO-254 hardware, ARP 4754A / ARP 4761), functions (PFD, ND, EWD/SD, MFD, alerts), manufacturing and tools (LRU, maintenance).

Slide 4: Non-emissive technology: LCD, an electronic light switch

Exploded LCD: backlight, rear vertical polariser, TFT glass, liquid crystal, RGB colour filter, front horizontal polariser; pixel OFF and ON; a pixel is three sub-pixels R, G, B; the TFT is a switch (gate line selects the row, data line carries video, the capacitor C_LC holds the voltage until the next refresh); brightness follows a voltage of 0 to 5 V; backlight (LED, light guide, prism, diffuser). Three alignment types: TN (cheap, narrow viewing angle, early aircraft), IPS (wide angle, good colour, current leader), MVA (high contrast, deep black, high-end cockpits).

Numbers: voltage 0 to 5 V

Slide 5: Resolution

Image size = width x height in pixels; a pixel consists of R, G, B. HD 1280x720 (0.9 MP), Full HD 1920x1080 (2.1 MP), 4K UHD 3840x2160 (8.3 MP). Resolution is pixels per inch (PPI) or pixel size (mm). To make pixels invisible, the angle of one pixel must be below the acuity of the eye (about 1 arcminute = 1/60 degree = 2.91e-4 rad). Example D = 5 m: h = D x theta = 5 x 2.91e-4 = 1.45e-3 m, about 1.45 mm.

Summary transcript from an earlier read of the slide image (10/2026); numbers not yet rechecked at high zoom.

Formulas: h = D x theta

Numbers: 1.45 mm at 5 m

Checked by computation: 2.91e-4 x 5 m = 1.45 mm; 1280x720 = 0.92 MP, 1920x1080 = 2.07 MP, 3840x2160 = 8.29 MP.

Slide 6: Visual acuity and the eye

1 arcmin = 1/60 degree = 0.0167 degree; tan(1/120 degree) = (h/2)/5 m, so h = 2 x 5 x tan(1/120 deg) about 1.45 mm. Anatomy of the eye (cornea, pupil, iris, lens, vitreous, retina: rods for dim colourless light, blue, green and red cones, optic nerve). Peak spectral sensitivity 555 nm; colour bands 470 nm blue, 532 nm green, 615 nm red. Table of smallest pixel by distance: 1 m 0.29 mm; 3 m 0.87 mm; 5 m 1.45 mm; 10 m 2.91 mm; 20 m 5.82 mm.

Numbers: 555 nm; 470, 532, 615 nm

Checked by computation: 2 x tan(1/120 degree) = 2.91e-4 so the values 0.29; 0.87; 1.45; 2.91; 5.82 mm agree.

Slide 7: Cockpit history

From no cockpit to the glass cockpit: 1903 Wright Flyer (no cockpit); 1918 simple cockpit (about 10 instruments); 1969 traditional cockpit (about 600 instruments, for example Boeing 707); 1970 Boeing 747; 1976 Concorde (advanced analogue); 1990s move to CRT; 2000s LCD glass (integrated A320/A330/A340); today large multifunction LCD/OLED touch displays (A350, Boeing 787). Key points: hundreds of instruments in the 1960s-1970s, analogue instruments from about 1918, then the move to digital (CRT then LCD).

(Odisis: one free multi-touch display). The cockpit is designed to simplify tasks, increase situational awareness, and increase safety and efficiency.

Slide 9: Cockpit Display System (ten blocks)

Slide 13: EFI (Electronic Flight Indicator)

The EFI shows flight parameters on a screen in place of electromechanical instruments; widely used on trainer and military aircraft (Embraer Tucano, KAI KT-1, HAL IJT 36, MiG 21, Tornado); sizes 3 ATI or 4 ATI; LCD. Structure: sensor data (ADIRU, air data, FMS/GPS) over ARINC 429, electronic processor, LCD (LED backlight, panel, polariser, glass), flight information output.

Summary transcript from an earlier read of the slide image (10/2026); numbers not yet rechecked at high zoom.

Slide 14: EIS = EFIS + ECAM

The EIS comprises the EFIS (PFD, ND) and the ECAM (EWD above, SD below). EFIS takes flight, navigation and control data; ECAM takes engine, system and warning data; both receive from the system computers (ADIRU, FMGC, OMS).
