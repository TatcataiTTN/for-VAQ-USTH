Data buses aircraft

A Vietnamese and English transcript of each slide, read directly from the image (18/18 slides). Module using this deck: M05. Where a slide prints something wrong or inconsistent it is listed under "Slide errors"; the transcript keeps what the slide prints.

Slide 1: Aircraft Systems, Analog Systems & Digital Systems: Data Buses

Title slide: "Aircraft Systems, Analog Systems & Digital Systems", subtitle "Data Buses", USTH logo and a glass cockpit photo.

Slide 2: Why do we need a data bus in an aircraft?

Left panel, no data bus: point-to-point connection; each sensor or computer (ADC, engine computer FADEC, landing gear sensor, fuel quantity sensor) is wired separately to every device that uses the data (EFIS, flight control computers ELAC/SEC/FAC, ECAM, FMS, other systems). Drawbacks: many cables, weight and complexity; difficult installation, maintenance and troubleshooting; hard to expand; high risk of wiring errors. Right panel, with a data bus: devices share one or more common buses (ARINC 429 basic avionics, ARINC 629 redundant, AFDX/ARINC 664 Ethernet, other buses MIL-STD-1553, ARINC 717); fewer cables, data shared among many devices, easier installation and fault isolation, easier expansion, standard protocols with error checking and bus monitoring. Bottom: six reasons: reduce wiring, weight and space; share data among devices; increase reliability and safety (Bus A primary, Bus B secondary); easier maintenance and fault isolation; easier expansion and upgrade; support several data types at suitable speed.

Slide 3: Data Buses in an Aircraft (Example: Airbus A320)

Overview diagram of an A320 with sensors, the ADIRU, EFIS (PFD/ND), FMS, ECAM, flight control computers (ELAC/SEC/FAC) with actuators, cabin systems (CIDS), ACMS and engines (FADEC), joined by coloured lines: green ARINC 429, blue ARINC 629, red AFDX/ARINC 664, orange other buses (MIL-STD-1553, ARINC 717). Four cards. 1 ARINC 429: simple bus, low speed, high reliability; point-to-point simplex (1 transmitter to several receivers); 12.5 or 100 kbps; 32-bit word; bipolar return to zero (BPRZ). 2 ARINC 629: multi-drop, dual-redundant (Bus A primary, Bus B secondary); 2 Mbps; TDMA access. 3 AFDX/ARINC 664: switched Ethernet, 100 Mbps full duplex, Virtual Links with guaranteed bandwidth. 4 MIL-STD-1553 / ARINC 717: 1553B is a dual-redundant command-response bus (bus controller, remote terminal, bus monitor), 1 Mbps, used with FADEC; ARINC 717 data rate "2/4/8 Mbps", mainly CVR/FDR recording. Footer: modern aircraft use several buses in parallel, each chosen by data rate, reliability, real-time performance and data volume.

Numbers: ARINC 429: 12.5 or 100 kbps, 32-bit words; ARINC 629: 2 Mbps; AFDX: 100 Mbps; MIL-STD-1553B: 1 Mbps; ARINC 717: 2/4/8 Mbps (theo slide)

Top groups: flight control (ELAC, SEC, FAC), flight management (FMGC, MCDU, FCU), air data/inertial (ADIRU), display/warning (DMC, DU, FWC), communication (VHF, HF, AMU, RMP). Bottom groups: navigation (MMR, DME, ADF, radio altimeter, weather radar), engine (EEC 1, 2), electrical (GCU, BCL, GPCU), air conditioning (pack and zone controllers), landing gear/brakes (LGCIU, BSCU), fuel, maintenance (CFDIU, DMU). Three boxes: bus legend (AFDX 100 Mbps switched redundant; ARINC 429 low speed 12.5/100 kbps point-to-point; ARINC 629 medium speed 2 Mbps dual redundant; discrete/analog signals; audio signals), example connections (for instance ADIRU to FMGC via ARINC 429, FMGC to MCDU/FCU via AFDX), typical LRU locations in the avionics compartments.

Slide 5: Airbus A320 - ARINC 629 Network (Only)

A diagram with ARINC 629 Bus A (redundant bus A) and Bus B (redundant bus B), each medium speed 2 Mbps, point-to-point with a bus coupler for each LRU. LRUs above and below the buses, each with Tx and Rx boxes: ELAC, SEC, FAC (flight control), FMGC (flight management), LGCIU (landing gear), BSCU (brakes), ADIRU, GCU, BCL, GPCU (electrical), EEC (engine), pack and zone controllers, fuel control unit, FWC, DMC, CFDIU, DMU, TCAS and weather radar (if installed). Legend: Tx = transmitter, Rx = receiver, bus coupling unit. Key points: dual redundant bus A and B; medium speed 2 Mbps; point-to-point architecture (daisy-chain) with bus couplers; each LRU can be transmitter, receiver or both; supports real-time system data; mainly used for system and sensor data between avionics LRUs (no AFDX, no ARINC 429 in this diagram). Example data flow: LGCIU (Tx) sends landing gear position data over ARINC 629 (2 Mbps) to DMC (Rx), which displays landing gear status.

Numbers: ARINC 629: 2 Mbps, Bus A and Bus B

Slide 6: ARINC 629 overview, architecture and characteristics

Eight panels. 1 Overview: ARINC 629 is a high-speed serial digital data bus standard developed for Airbus aircraft (A320, A330, A340, A380) by ARINC; it lets several LRUs communicate through two independent buses A and B with real-time access using TDMA; data rate 2 Mbps; used for central avionics systems with high data needs. 2 Network architecture on A320: ADIRU, FMGS, ELAC, FAC, SEC, ECAM on bus A (2 Mbps) and bus B (2 Mbps), plus ACMS, CFDIU, CIDS, SDAC, ATC/TCAS and other LRUs. 3 Physical structure: each bus with a 78 ohm termination at both ends, LRU 1 to LRU n; transmission differential, twisted and shielded pair. 4 Message frame: Sync 8 bits, Data 32 bits (4 bytes), SDI 2 bits, Label 8 bits, SSM 2 bits, total 52 bits. 5 TDMA channel access: a major frame of 125 slots of 16 us each, major frame cycle 2 ms; each LRU transmits in its assigned slot. 6 Example waveform: Bus A (+) and Bus A (-) between +5 V and -5 V, differential voltage Vdiff = 10 V. 7 Key characteristics table: 2 Mbps, differential twisted shielded pair, two buses, TDMA, frame length 52 bits, 125 slots per major frame, slot duration 16 us, major frame cycle 2 ms, up to 20-21 LRUs on one bus. 8 Applications on A320: ELAC/SEC/FAC to ADIRU for attitude and speed; FMGS to ECAM for flight phase and warnings; others.

Formulas: 125 x 16 us = 2 ms · 8 + 32 + 2 + 8 + 2 = 52 bit

Numbers: 2 Mbps; 78 ohm; 52 bit; 125 khe x 16 us = 2 ms; +5 V / -5 V, Vdiff = 10 V

Slide errors and doubts
  • Does not match Tooley (p.66, section 4.3.6): ARINC 629 supports 120 terminals on a bus and is used on the Boeing 777, Airbus A330 and A340; the slide says "at most 20-21 LRUs on a bus" and adds the A320 and A380.

Checked by computation: 125 slots x 16 us = 2 ms and 8 + 32 + 2 + 8 + 2 = 52 bits both agree.

Slide 7: ARINC 664 (AFDX): overview, frame, virtual link, bandwidth

Seven panels. 1 Overview: ARINC 664 (Part 7) defines Ethernet technology for aircraft; AFDX is its implementation, used on Airbus (A380, A350, and some systems on A320); switched Ethernet at 100 Mbps full duplex; quality of service through Virtual Links and deterministic communication; real-time and safety-critical; carries large data such as images, video, maintenance data. 2 AFDX network architecture on an A350 example: ADIRU, FMS, CFDIU, CIDS, IFE and a maintenance system joined to redundant AFDX switches, with flight control computers, ECAM display, cabin systems, brakes and engines (FADEC) on the switches, and external connections (SATCOM, airport network, ground data loading). 3 Frame structure based on Ethernet (64 to 1518 bytes): preamble 7 bytes, SFD 1 byte, destination MAC 6, source MAC 6, EtherType 2, payload 46 to 1500 bytes (virtual link data), FCS 4 bytes (CRC 32). 4 Virtual Link: a logical connection between a source and a destination; each VL has its own bandwidth, frame size, transmission period (BAG) and priority; the switch isolates VLs by per-VL policing. 5 Bandwidth and QoS: BAG is the minimum interval between two consecutive frames of a VL; it limits bandwidth and prevents congestion. 6 Key characteristics: 100 Mbps, switched Ethernet full duplex, VL plus BAG, frame length 64 to 1518 bytes, deterministic, two independent networks A and B, typical number of VLs up to 20-21 (as read). 7 Advantages and applications on Airbus.

Numbers: 100 Mbps; frame 64 to 1518 bytes; preamble 7 + SFD 1 + MAC 6 + 6 + EtherType 2 + payload 46 to 1500 + FCS 4

Slide errors and doubts
  • Doubt: "typical number of VLs up to 20-21" (read at low zoom, small print) looks too small for an AFDX network; the original slide must be reread at high resolution before use.

Checked by computation: 7 + 1 + 6 + 6 + 2 + 46 + 4 = 72 bytes including the preamble and SFD, i.e. a minimum ethernet frame of 64 bytes (not counting the preamble and SFD); it agrees with "64 to 1518".

Slide 10: ARINC 429 - Data communication standard in aircraft

Nine panels. 1 Overview: point-to-point data standard developed by ARINC, used in Airbus A320 and Boeing 737/777; data rate 12.5 kbps (mainly) or 100 kbps; one transmitter can connect to at most 20 receivers; differential signalling with bipolar return to zero (BPRZ) encoding; shielded twisted pair. 2 Example connection: ADIRU transmits over ARINC 429 (one way) to EFIS (PFD/ND), FMS and other systems; one transmitter can feed up to 20 receivers but only one direction. 3 Cable: differential signalling on two wires A and B of opposite polarity, reduces EMI, shielded; typical cable impedance 70 to 85 ohm (usually 78 ohm). 4 Data word format, total 32 bits (4 bytes), Label 8 bits (bit 1 to 8), SDI 2 bits, Data 19 bits, SSM 2 bits, Parity 1 bit; label identifies data type (for example 203 = IAS), parity is "even parity". 5 BPRZ encoding: bit 1: Tx+ positive and Tx- negative (about +-10 V) then both return to 0 V in the middle of the bit period; bit 0: Tx+ negative and Tx- positive then return to 0 V. 6 Example, transmit airspeed IAS: label 203 (0xCB), SDI 0, data 0x1F4 (for example 500 kt, depending on format), SSM 00 normal operation, even parity bit; label 1100 1011, SDI 00, data 00000011110100, SSM 00, P 0. 7 Data rate and bit timing: 12.5 kbps gives bit time 80 us and word time 2.56 ms; 100 kbps gives 10 us and 0.32 ms. 8 SSM values (example): 00 normal operation (valid data), 01 no computed data (not valid), 10 functional test (in test mode), 11 failure (fault). 9 Advantages and disadvantages.

Formulas: 32 x 80 us = 2,56 ms · 32 x 10 us = 0,32 ms

Numbers: 12.5 kbps: bit 80 us, word 2.56 ms; 100 kbps: bit 10 us, word 0.32 ms; at most 20 receivers; cable impedance 70 to 85 ohms (usually 78 ohms)

  • Does not match Tooley: the slide writes "label 203 (0xCB)" as a decimal number converted to hex; Tooley p.62 (Ch.4): the label is an octal value; the standard uses odd parity. If 203 is octal it corresponds to 1000 0011.
  • Internally inconsistent: the SSM table (00 normal, 11 failed) is the reverse of slides 15 and 17 of the same deck (00 failed, 11 normal, for BNR data). The Tooley in the folder has no SSM table so this cannot be settled from the sources.

Checked by computation: 32 x 80 us = 2.56 ms; 32 x 10 us = 0.32 ms; 0xCB = 1100 1011; 0x1F4 = 500 decimal.

Slide 11: Why can one LRU be both a transmitter (Tx) and a receiver (Rx) in ARINC 429?

An LRU in an A320 usually needs to send its own data to other LRUs (act as Tx) and receive data from others (act as Rx), so most LRUs have multiple channels. ARINC 429 is a point-to-point unidirectional bus: one Tx to multiple Rx; to exchange data between two LRUs two separate channels are needed: channel 1 LRU A transmits to LRU B (A to B), channel 2 LRU B transmits to LRU A (B to A); so an LRU usually has both Tx and Rx channels. Number of channels: each LRU can have several Tx and Rx channels; an ADIRU can have many Tx channels and many Rx channels; the number depends on the LRU design. The same LRU-group diagram with Bus A and Bus B as slide 9. Example 1: ADIRU to FMGC (channel 1, air data and IR data) and FMGC to ADIRU (channel 2, mode selection, input data). Example 2: MCDU as transmitter (flight plan data, INIT data, pilot commands, maintenance test commands) and receiver (data from FMGC, CFDs, status information). Example 3: ADIRU with channels Tx1 air data (ADR) to FMGC, FWC, DMC, ECAM; Tx2 inertial data (IR); Tx3 heading/attitude; Rx1 selected modes from FMGC; Rx2 IRS alignment command from MCDU/CFDIU; Rx3 maintenance data from CFDIU; Rx4 other commands.

Slide 12: Example: ARINC 429 data transmission between transmitter and receiver in A320

Six panels on sending indicated airspeed (IAS) from ADIRU to PFD. 1 Connection: pitot and static pressure sensors feed the ADIRU (transmitter) which sends over a one-way ARINC 429 bus to the PFD (receiver), shown on the airspeed tape. 2 Data to be transmitted: parameter IAS, label (hex) 215 (for example using label 215), value 250 kt, data format BCD, source ADIRU, receiver PFD and other devices. 3 Data encoding: 250 kt in BCD, 2 gives 0010, 5 gives 0101, 0 gives 0000; 19-bit data 0000 0000 0010 0101 000 (remaining bits 0); label 215 (hex) = 1000 0101 (binary); SDI = 00 (sent to all receivers). 4 Word structure (32 bits): Label (8 bits) 215 (1000 0101), SDI 00, Data (19 bits) BCD for 250 kt, SSM 00 (normal), Parity P; complete word 1000 0101 00 0000 0000 0010 0101 000 00 P. 5 Transmission on the bus: continuous transmission of ARINC 429 words (for example every 100 ms) on a twisted-shielded pair at +-10 V, 12.5 or 100 kbps. 6 Decoding at the receiver: receive the signal, check parity, decode label (215 = indicated airspeed), data (BCD 250 = 250 kt) and SSM (00 = valid data), update the PFD (250 kt shown). Notes: one-way bus; the same data can be received by several devices; each parameter has its own label and uses BCD or BNR.

Numbers: IAS 250 kt = BCD 0010 0101 0000; from 1000 0101 00 0000 0000 0010 0101 000 00 P

Slide errors and doubts
  • Mismatch inside the slide (confirmed at 4x zoom): "Label 215 (hex) = 1000 0101 (binary)". 1000 0101 is 0x85, i.e. 133 decimal or 205 octal; 215 hex needs 10 bits; 215 octal is 1000 1101. No reading of "215" gives 1000 0101.
  • Does not match Tooley: Tooley p.62 (Ch.4): the label is an octal value; the standard uses odd parity. (The slide only writes "P" without saying even or odd on this slide.)

Checked by computation: 8 + 2 + 19 + 2 + 1 = 32 bits and the data field 0000 0000 0010 0101 000 has exactly 19 digits.

Slide 13: ARINC 429 - Bipolar Return-to-Zero (BPRZ) signal: why does each bit return to zero?

Six panels. 1 Voltage levels: HI (1) is +5 V on wire A and -5 V on wire B, differential +10 V, meaning bit 1; NULL is 0 V on both, differential 0 V, no data; LO (0) is -5 V on A and +5 V on B, differential -10 V, bit 0; the receiver only cares about the differential voltage VA - VB. 2 Waveform of each bit: bit 1, bit 0 and NULL drawn against bit time. 3 Example: the bit sequence 1 0 1 1 0 0 1 0 shown on wire A, wire B and the differential voltage (+10 V to -10 V). 4 Comparison with and without return to zero: NRZ (not used in ARINC 429) holds +10 V for a long sequence of 1s with no transitions, hard to synchronise; BPRZ (used in ARINC 429) returns to 0 V each bit, a transition in every bit, easy to synchronise. 5 Gap between words: after 32 bits the transmitter keeps the NULL level for at least 4 bit-times. 6 Advantages of return-to-zero: self-clocking; clear separation between consecutive bits; clear gap between words (NULL for at least 4 bit-times after 32 bits); reduces the impact of DC offset and improves noise immunity; NULL is an intermediate state, not a valid bit value.

Numbers: HI: A +5 V, B -5 V, vi sai +10 V; LO: A -5 V, B +5 V, vi sai -10 V; NULL gap between words: at least 4 bit times

Slide errors and doubts
  • This slide describes BPRZ correctly; slides 14 and 15 of the same deck write "Manchester Biphase-L" for ARINC 429 (see those slides).

Slide 14: ARINC 429 - Bit encoding example and electrical characteristics

Four panels. 1 Bit encoding (Fig. 1): bit 1 (high) and bit 0 (low) drawn on Line A and Line B over 10 us; text: ARINC 429 uses Manchester Biphase-L encoding; each bit has a transition at the middle of the bit period (5 us); bit 1: Line A at high level (+V), Line B low (-V) for the first half of the bit; bit 0: the reverse; each bit lasts 10 us, corresponding to 100 kbps (or 12.5 kbps). 2 Electrical characteristics table: Line A +5 V +-0.5 V, Line B -5 V +-0.5 V (relative to ground), differential voltage 10 V nominal, encoding Manchester Biphase-L (Bipolar Return to Zero), word length 32 bits, high data rate 100 kbps (bit time 10 us), low data rate 12.5 kbps (bit time 80 us), up to 20 receivers per transmitter, shielded twisted pair, maximum cable length about 200 m, unidirectional point-to-point, error code NRZ (non-return to zero) at fault level. 3 Structure of the 32-bit data word (Fig. 2) drawn with bit 32 at the left: P (bit 32, even parity, 1 bit), Label (8 bits, 0 to 255), Data field (19 bits), SDI (2 bits, 0 to 3), SSM (2 bits). 4 Example word (Fig. 3): P 0, Label 8D (1000 1101), Data 1A3F5 (000 1101000 0111110101), SDI 1 (01), SSM 2 (10); a differential waveform over time, bit time 10 us at 100 kbps.

Numbers: Line A +5 V +-0,5 V; Line B -5 V +-0,5 V; vi sai 10 V; 100 kbps = 10 us; 12,5 kbps = 80 us; cable up to about 200 m

Slide errors and doubts
  • Inconsistent internally and with Tooley: the slide writes the ARINC 429 coding as "Manchester Biphase-L (Bipolar Return to Zero)", while slides 3 and 13 of the same deck describe BPRZ; Tooley (Electrical Characteristics) also uses BPRZ. Manchester and BPRZ are two different codes (Module 05 already records this error).
  • Does not match Tooley: the slide says even parity; Tooley p.62 (Ch.4): the label is an octal value; the standard uses odd parity.
  • Inconsistent within the deck: figure 2 draws the bit order 32 (P), label at bits 31 to 24, data, SDI, SSM at bits 2 to 1; slides 10, 15, 16, 17 and Tooley (p.62) put the label at bits 1 to 8, SDI at bits 9 and 10, data at bits 11 to 29, SSM at bits 30 and 31, parity at bit 32.

Checked by computation: 8D hex = 1000 1101; 1A3F5 hex needs 17 bits (1 1010 0011 1111 0101), not 19 bits like the data field; the example mixes notations. bit time at 100 kbps = 10 us; at 12.5 kbps = 80 us.

Slide 15: Word format of ARINC 429

Six panels. 1 Structure of a 32-bit word (Fig. 1): bits numbered 1 to 32, LSB transmitted first, MSB transmitted last; drawn as Label (8 bits), Data (19 bits), SDI (2 bits), Parity (1 bit), SSM (2 bits) from left to right. Transmission characteristics: each bit uses Manchester (bi-phase) coding; the most significant bit (bit 1 - LSB) is transmitted first and the least significant (bit 32 - MSB) last; data rate 12.5 kbps (low speed) or 100 kbps (high speed). 2 Fields table: Label 8 bits, Data 19, SDI 2, Parity 1 (odd parity bit for error detection), SSM 2. 3 Meaning of each field: Label (8 bits) identifies the type of data, values 0 to 255 (hex); Data (19 bits) signed, unsigned, BCD, binary, discrete; SDI (2 bits) source or destination, lets several devices share the bus using the label; Parity (1 bit) odd parity bit (total number of 1s in 32 bits is odd); SSM (2 bits) status of the data (00 failure, 01 no computed data, 10 functional test, 11 normal operation). 4 SSM status (example): 00 Failure Warning; 01 No Computed Data (data not valid); 10 Functional Test (system test in progress); 11 Normal Operation (data valid). 5 Transmission order: bit 1 is transmitted first, bit 32 last; order of fields on the bus from first to last: 8 (Label), 19 (Data), 2 (SDI), 1 (Parity), 2 (SSM). 6 Example word: Label 0110 1001 (8 bits), Data 19 bits, SDI 01, Parity 1, SSM 11. Notes: each system defines its own label and data format; the bit with the lowest index is sent first; bit time and voltage levels are defined in the ARINC 429 standard.

Slide errors and doubts
  • Does not match Tooley: the slide draws the field order Label, Data, SDI, Parity, SSM (SDI after the data, parity at bit 30, SSM at bits 31 and 32). Tooley Figure 4.11 (p.62) and slides 10, 16, 17 put: label bits 1 to 8, SDI bits 9 and 10, data bits 11 to 29, SSM bits 30 and 31, parity bit 32.
  • Internally inconsistent: the slide says Manchester code (two-phase) while slide 13 describes BPRZ and Tooley uses BPRZ.
  • Internally inconsistent: the sentence "the most significant bit (bit 1 - LSB) is sent first" contradicts itself (bit 1 is the LSB).
  • This slide gives odd parity, matching Tooley (p.62); slides 10 and 14 say even.

Slide 16: ARINC 429 - Data types (BCD and BNR)

Seven panels. 1 Structure of the 32-bit word with bit 32 at the left: P (parity, even parity, 1 bit), SSM (2 bits, bits 31 and 30), Data (19 bits), SDI (2 bits), Label (8 bits). 2 BCD encoding: 4 bits per decimal digit; the 19-bit data field can hold up to 5 decimal digits (5 x 4 = 20 bits with 1 bit unused); the most significant bit of the 19-bit field is unused (Sign) and the remaining 18 bits are used for digits; example encoding the number 19; fields SD (scale/discretisation, decimal point position), S (sign: 0 positive, 1 negative), CHAR 1 to CHAR 5 (4 bits each); table decimal digit to BCD code 0000 to 1001. 3 Example of a BCD data word: transmit +257 (5 digits, SD = 00) with CHAR1 0, CHAR2 2, CHAR3 5, CHAR4 7, CHAR5 0 (as read). 4 BNR encoding: signed integer in two's complement using 19 bits; bit 29 sign bit (0 positive, 1 negative); bit 28 used for SD or reserved; bits 27 to 11 magnitude field (17 bits); example encoding the value +100 (BNR, SD = 00). 5 SSM for BCD data: 00 plus, north, east, right, up, above (valid data, positive); 01 no computed data (data not valid); 10 functional test (in test mode); 11 minus, south, west, left, from, below (valid data, negative). 6 SDI (bits 10 and 9): 00 to 11 give source/destination 0 to 3. 7 Label (bits 8 to 1): 8-bit label identifies the data type, value 0 to 255 (defined by ARINC); common labels (decimal): 203 indicated airspeed (true air speed), 204 Mach number, 205 pressure altitude.

Slide errors and doubts
  • Does not match Tooley: Tooley p.62 (Ch.4): the label is an octal value; the standard uses odd parity. The slide says even parity and gives the labels in decimal (203, 204, 205). Tooley Figure 4.13 (p.63) uses label 103 for the selected speed.
  • Internally inconsistent: label 203 is written "Indicated Airspeed (True Air Speed)", mixing two different concepts (indicated and true airspeed).
  • Doubt: the BCD example +257 with SD = 00 sets the CHARs to 0, 2, 5, 7, 0 (read from a small image), which looks like 02570 rather than 257; to be reread at high resolution.

Slide 17: ARINC 429 - Data formats (BNR example, sign representation)

Five panels. 1 General format of a 32-bit word (Fig. 6, generalised BNR word format) drawn with bit 32 at the left: P (1 bit), SSM (2 bits), Data (19 bits), Pad (1 bit), SDI (2 bits), Label (8 bits); field table: Label 8 bits identifies the data type and meaning; SDI 2 bits source or destination; Pad 1 bit padding (usually 0); Data 19 bits actual data (numeric, BCD, characters); SSM 2 bits (normal, fail, no computed data); P (Parity) 1 bit odd parity so that the total number of 1 bits in the word is odd. 2 BNR example: the value 103 (positive) encoded in the 19-bit data field: label 103 (example label), SDI 00 (source 0), Pad 0, Data 19 bits "000 0000 1100111" (as read) for 103 decimal, bit 29 = 0 positive, SSM 11 (normal operation, valid data), P (Parity) = 0 chosen so the total number of 1s is odd. 3 Sign representation in BNR: two's complement for signed integers with 19 bits; bit 29 is the sign bit (0 positive, 1 negative); the numeric value is computed from the 19-bit field by the label definition (scale factor); unused bits are filled with 0. 4 Other data formats: BCD, character data, discrete data, mixed formats; labels 29 to 11 are used (as read). 5 Important notes: each label has a specific definition (data type, scale factor, meaning); bit 32 (parity) is always transmitted last; common SSM values 00 failure warning, 01 no computed data, 10 functional test, 11 normal operation.

Slide errors and doubts
  • Arithmetic mismatch (read from the image): figure 6 lists P 1 + SSM 2 + Data 19 + Pad 1 + SDI 2 + Label 8 = 33 bits, not 32 like slides 10, 15, 16 (8 + 2 + 19 + 2 + 1).
  • Doubt: the string "000 0000 1100111" for 103 has only 14 digits while the data field is 19 bits (read at low resolution); 103 = 1100111 (7 bits).
  • The slide gives odd parity, matching Tooley (p.62), and the SSM table matches slide 15 (00 failed, 11 normal). Slide 10 says the opposite.
