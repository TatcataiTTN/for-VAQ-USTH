Source errors and doubts

Collected from the module notes. Each entry says which source is wrong and which value the site used. Not yet fully rechecked against page images: see CONTINUE.md.

M01 · Number systems in aircraft digital data

⚠️ Common trap

The most common mistake is converting directly between hex and octal without going through binary. Because hex groups bits in 4s while octal groups them in 3s, the two groupings do NOT line up: always expand to full binary first, then regroup for the target base, as illustrated in question 9 below (111₁₆ → 421₈ cannot be inferred directly).

M02 · Logic gates and Boolean algebra

⚠️ Common trap

Confusing NAND/NOR with 'the everyday negation of AND/OR' by guessing the truth table intuitively instead of computing it step by step: the safest approach is always to write out the full AND/OR truth table first, then invert the result bit by bit, never skip straight to the answer.

M03 · Integrated circuits and multiplexing

⚠️ Common trap

Confusing a 'demultiplexer' (1 input → many outputs) with a 'decoder' (address decoding): the two sometimes share the same physical IC (a decoder with no dedicated data input can act as a demux by treating one line as data and the rest as select address), but functionally they serve different purposes.

M04 · Computer structure and microprocessors (CPU)

⚠️ Common trap

Confusing 'random access memory' (the technical meaning: every cell is accessed with equal ease) with RAM (read/write memory) in everyday usage. Technically, ROM is ALSO random-access memory (not sequential like magnetic tape), even though it cannot be written to.

M05 · Aircraft data buses

⚠️ Common trap (and a real error found in the lecture material itself)

Common trap: confusing ARINC 429's top speed of 100 kbps with the Mbps-range speeds of ARINC 629 (2 Mbps) or MIL-STD-1553 (1 Mbps): these are three different standards, tens of times apart in speed. More notably: one slide in this very course's lecture deck states that ARINC 429 uses "Manchester Biphase-L" encoding, but cross-checking against Tooley (the Electrical Characteristics section, and the BPRZ glossary entry) and two OTHER slides in that SAME lecture deck shows the CORRECT encoding for ARINC 429 is Bipolar Return to Zero (BPRZ), not Manchester (Manchester is used by ARINC 708/573, a DIFFERENT bus standard). This is a real example of why cross-checking multiple sources matters before memorising a technical fact.

M06 · Electronic displays and flight deck information systems

⚠️ Common traps (and points in the lecture that need a critical reading)

General trap: mixing up N1 and N2. The lecture's E/WD slide calls N2 the "low pressure turbine speed" while the same slide calls N1 the low pressure spool; Tooley (section 1.1.6) states that N1 is fan speed and N2 is high pressure turbine speed, so N2 must be the high pressure one. The ACARS slide expands SDAC as "System Data Analog Computer" but the EIS diagram slide says "System Data Acquisition Concentrator" (the second matches the function: it acquires data). The ECAM ELEC slide draws generators at "420 V, 200 Hz" while aircraft AC power is 115 V, 400 Hz (Tooley mentions a 115 V AC 400 Hz supply). The PRESS slide at FL140 shows a differential pressure of 2.9 psi together with a cabin altitude of 2,870 ft; in the ISA standard atmosphere the differential pressure should be about 4.6 psi (see notebook), so these are illustrative numbers and should not be memorised. On the ND slide, "23 33" is labelled as time to waypoint (23 min 33 s), yet 37 NM at a ground speed of 257 kt takes only about 8.6 minutes; it is probably an estimated time of arrival (ETA) as a UTC clock time, not yet checked against manufacturer documents, so it is flagged as doubtful. The A310 slide says "EICAS" while Airbus's name, per Tooley, is ECAM. Finally, a genuine printing error in Tooley: the chapter 11 answer key gives question 4 as (a), but the human eye is most sensitive to green light (peak near 550 nm, exactly as question 2 and lecture slide 6 say), so the correct answer is (c); the site uses the correct answer and says so. On the Floyd side, the printed answer to Problem 67, chapter 4 (the circuit in Figure P-19) cannot light segment a for the letter A (checked with the full truth table), so it is not used as the reference answer.

M07 · Aircraft software management control

⚠️ Common traps (and where the lecture notes are incomplete)

General trap: believing that a checksum or CRC proves the software is correct. They only detect that a file changed; Tooley states plainly that neither method is completely fool-proof (see Demo 1: swapping two bytes or two cancelling errors fool the checksum). Second trap: mixing up FLS and DFLD; DFLD is data, but it still needs its own part number and release documentation like executable code. One place where the lecture notes are incomplete: Question 13-6 asks which trace is required only at level A, and the printed answer names only source code to executable object code, yet the notes' own Figure 13-1 draws two red arrows (level A only): besides source code to object code there is also software architecture to high-level requirements. Tooley itself uses both "Level" (Table 13.1) and "Class" (multiple-choice question 4) for the same concept. On the Floyd side: the CRC division in section 2-12 appends as many zeros as the generator has bits (4 bits means 4 zeros), whereas the standard CRC appends the generator length minus one; both give a zero remainder on a correct reception, so this is a difference of presentation, not an error.

M08 · Fibre optics in aircraft

⚠️ Common traps (and mismatches found in the material)

General trap: mixing up the critical angle θc (computed at the core-cladding boundary) with the acceptance angle θa (computed at the fibre input, from air). With n₁ = 1.53 and n₂ = 1.50, θc = 78.6° but θa is only 17.5°. That is why slide 19, which writes "θ₁ = θc = θa", is wrong: the two angles are measured in different places and are not equal; the correct limiting condition is that the angle φ at the boundary (measured from the normal) equals θc, equivalently that the ray angle to the fibre axis inside equals 90° − θc. The symbol θ₁ is also used differently on slide 17 (angle in air) and slide 19 (angle in the core). Slide 9 lists water with ε_r ≈ 80 but n ≈ 1.33; √80 ≈ 8.9, not 1.33: ε_r = 80 is the static permittivity, while at light frequencies ε_r ≈ 1.77 (= 1.33²). Slide 28 writes Δt ≈ (n₁ − n₂)L/c, missing a factor n₁/n₂ (the correct expression for the critical ray is n₁(n₁ − n₂)L/(c n₂)), and gives a typical 10 to 50 ns/km whereas n₁ − n₂ = 0.02 to 0.04 gives about 67 to 137 ns/km. Slide 30 labels the graded-index fibre "MM-SI" while the comparison table below says MM-GI. Slide 24 gives 0.2 to 0.5 dB/km at 1550 nm while slide 26 gives 0.18 dB/km. Finally, Tooley section 10.2 states the refraction rule backwards: the book says that going from higher to lower refractive index the ray is "bent towards the normal", but by Snell's law it bends away from the normal (lecture slide 12 states it correctly); the book's wording contradicts Snell's law and the lecture, so do not use it for study.

M09 · Electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) in aircraft

⚠️ Common traps (and mismatches found in the material)

General trap: confusing 0 dBm with 0 W (0 dBm is 1 mW) and mixing the factor 10 with 20 in the dB formulas (10 for power, 20 for voltage and current). Mismatches: slide 21 (exercise 2) prints "P_out = 10000 W (10 kW)" although the calculation right next to it gives 100,000 W; the correct answer is 100 kW because 10 W × 10⁴ = 100,000 W (see Demo 1 and the notebook). Slide 43 requires a bonding resistance of 0.001 to 0.003 Ω, whereas slide 45 says "typically below 2.5 mΩ" and the tester picture there reads 0.003 Ω, i.e. 3 mΩ, above 2.5 mΩ and therefore failing by that slide's own rule; Tooley says below 0.001 to 0.003 Ω and quiz question 6 says below 0.005 Ω, so the sources use different thresholds and the manufacturer's maintenance data must be followed. Tooley (Figure 14.14) says the noise floor rose by 26 dB "or 50 times"; 26 dB is about 20 times in amplitude (about 400 times in power), not 50. Slide 10 and Tooley 14.1.2 write the square wave as V sin ωt + (V/3) sin 3ωt + …; that is a simplified scaled form, since the fundamental of a square wave of amplitude V is really 4V/π, so read V there as the fundamental amplitude, not the square-wave amplitude. Slide 1 misspells "Dndergoes" in the Global Hawk caption, slide 16 repeats slide 15, and the slide 2 heading "Historical facts" has no content. On the Floyd side: Floyd has no EMC chapter; it mentions EMI only at cable level (twisted pairs reduce crosstalk, shielded STP resists EMI), board-level decoupling capacitors, and the noise margin of logic families.
