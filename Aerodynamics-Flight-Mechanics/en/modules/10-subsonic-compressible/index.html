MODULE 10 · Subsonic compressible

Subsonic compressible flow past airfoils: the Prandtl-Glauert rule and the critical Mach number

Module 03 said that near the speed of sound compressibility changes lift and causes shocks, but not by how much. This module gives the numbers: for subsonic flow past a thin airfoil the pressure and lift coefficients are multiplied by 1/√(1 − M²) (the Prandtl-Glauert rule). Kármán-Tsien and Laitone are more accurate corrections. Combined with the critical pressure coefficient this gives the critical Mach number, the point at which supersonic flow first appears locally on the wing.

Anderson ch. 11 (sec. 11.1–11.6; Problems 11.1–11.7) · EASA Module 08, Sub-module 03 (pp. 3.10–3.11)

PART 1/5
01

The Prandtl-Glauert rule

  • C_p = C_p,0/√(1 − M∞²), c_l = c_l,0/√(1 − M∞²), c_m = c_m,0/√(1 − M∞²)
  • For thin airfoils at small angle of attack, M∞ not too near 1
  • The factor grows: 1.25 at M = 0.6; 1.40 at M = 0.7; 1.67 at M = 0.8

In plain words
Compressible flow "amplifies" every pressure disturbance: an airfoil lifts more and sucks harder as the speed approaches that of sound.
PART 2/5
02

The Kármán-Tsien and Laitone corrections

  • Kármán-Tsien: C_p = C_p,0/[√(1 − M²) + (M²/(1 + √(1 − M²)))·C_p,0/2]
  • Laitone: C_p = C_p,0/[√(1 − M²) + (M²(1 + (γ−1)M²/2)/(2√(1 − M²)))·C_p,0]
  • Both include nonlinearity so they give a larger |C_p| than Prandtl-Glauert

Matches the Solution Manual: −0.663; −0.7063; −0.7763. The three rules differ by about 17%. According to the Solution Manual, experience shows Kármán-Tsien to be the most accurate of the three.

In plain words
Prandtl-Glauert is linear so it underestimates the suction; the other two add the nonlinear effect.
PART 3/5
03

Critical pressure coefficient and critical Mach number

  • The critical Mach number M_cr is the M∞ at which the point of strongest suction reaches local M = 1
  • There p/p∞ = (p₀/p∞)/(p₀/p at M = 1) and C_p,cr = 2/(γM²)·(p/p∞ − 1)
  • M_cr is the intersection of the Prandtl-Glauert curve with the C_p,cr(M) curve
PART 3/5 · 10

Problems 11.4 and 11.5

  • 11.4: C_p,0 = −0.41 gives M_cr = 0.744 (the Solution Manual plots the curves and reads M_cr = 0.74)
  • 11.5: with M_cr = 0.8 the pressure ratio at the minimum-pressure point is p/p∞ = 0.805 (Solution Manual 0.805)
  • A thinner airfoil with gentler suction (small |C_p,0|) has a higher M_cr. That is one reason jet-aircraft wings are thin and swept
In plain words
Passing M_cr does not mean a strong shock at once. Wave drag rises sharply only at the drag-divergence Mach number, 5–10% above M_cr (EASA p. 3.14).
PART 4/5
04

From local Mach number to pressure coefficient

  • In compressible flow C_p is a function of the local and free-stream Mach numbers, not of velocity
  • C_p = (2/γM∞²)·[((1 + 0.2M∞²)/(1 + 0.2M²))^3.5 − 1]
  • A generalisation of the incompressible C_p = 1 − (V/V∞)²
PART 4/5 · 10

Problems 11.1, 11.6 and 11.7

  • 11.1: at (0.2, 0.2) ft: V = 781.5 ft/s, M = 0.707, p = 0.934 atm, T = 508.9 °R
  • 11.6: M∞ = 0.5, local M = 0.86: p/p∞ = 0.7319, C_p = −1.532 (Solution Manual −1.53)
  • 11.7: starting from M∞ = 0.3 with M_A = 0.435 ⇒ C_p,0 = −0.989; Prandtl-Glauert takes M_A to 0.772 (M∞ = 0.5), 0.982 (0.6) and 1.006 at M∞ = 0.61: so M_cr ≈ 0.61
In plain words
The value M_cr = 0.61 in the book's Figure 11.5 is not arbitrary: it is the result of the calculation in Problem 11.7.

History

Prandtl, Glauert and wartime

Anderson writes that the Prandtl-Glauert rule appears not only in the work of Ludwig Prandtl (about 1922) and Hermann Glauert (1928) but also in earlier studies. He also notes the need for more accurate corrections as aircraft speeds soared in World War II; the Kármán-Tsien rule has been widely used in the industry since then (Anderson 11.5).

Case

Case: airfoil lift at M = 0.6

  • A NACA 2412 airfoil at α = 5° has c_l,0 = 0.75 at low speed (the Solution Manual reads it from Figure 4.5; interpolating the table of Problem 1.6 gives 0.745).
  • At M = 0.6, c_l = 0.75/√(1 − 0.36) = 0.938: 25% more from compressibility alone (Problem 11.2).
  • At the same angle of attack a faster aircraft lifts more, so the trim attitude changes with Mach number.
Pitfalls

Common pitfalls

  • Prandtl-Glauert holds only for M∞ below M_cr and for thin airfoils. Near M = 1 it gives infinity, which does not reflect reality.
  • In compressible flow C_p depends on the local Mach number, not on velocity (Problem 11.6).
  • The correction applies to C_p,0, c_l,0 and c_m,0, not to drag.
  • M_cr is below 1: the aircraft is still subsonic when supersonic flow first appears locally on the wing.
  • Problem 11.2 needs c_l,0 from Figure 4.5. The module uses 0.75 as the Solution Manual does; interpolating the table of Problem 1.6 gives 0.745 (0.931 instead of 0.938).

Worked problems

Problems 11.1–11.7 all agree with the 4th-edition Solution Manual. Problem 11.7 differs slightly because the Solution Manual interpolates tables; the conclusion M_cr ≈ 0.61 is the same.

11.3. In incompressible flow the pressure coefficient at a point on an airfoil is −0.54. Find C_p at that point when M∞ = 0.58 using (a) the Prandtl-Glauert rule, (b) the Kármán-Tsien rule, (c) Laitone's rule.

Anderson 6th, Problem 11.3

Show the solution

What the problem says. The same low-speed value is corrected by three different formulas.

Idea. Compute β = √(1 − M²) = 0.8146 first and substitute into each formula.

Steps.

  1. β = √(1 − 0.58²) = 0.8146; M² = 0.3364.
  2. (a) C_p = −0.54/0.8146 = −0.663.
  3. (b) Denominator = 0.8146 + (0.3364/1.8146)(−0.54/2) = 0.7646 ⇒ C_p = −0.7063.
  4. (c) Denominator = 0.8146 + [0.3364 × (1 + 0.2 × 0.3364)/(2 × 0.8146)](−0.54) = 0.6956 ⇒ C_p = −0.7763.

Check: Solution Manual: −0.663; −0.7063; −0.7763 (about 17% spread between the three rules).

11.4 – 11.5. (11.4) In incompressible flow the peak pressure coefficient (at the minimum-pressure point) of an airfoil is −0.41. Estimate the critical Mach number with the Prandtl-Glauert rule. (11.5) An airfoil has M_cr = 0.8. Find p/p∞ at the minimum-pressure point when M∞ = 0.8.

Anderson 6th, Problems 11.4, 11.5

Show the solution

What the problem says. M_cr is the M∞ that brings the strongest-suction point to M = 1.

Idea. (11.4) Two curves C_p(M) intersect: Prandtl-Glauert and C_p,cr. (11.5) When M∞ = M_cr the local M is 1 so use the isentropic pressure ratio.

Steps.

  1. C_p,cr(M) = (2/γM²)[((1 + 0.2M²)/1.2)^3.5 − 1].
  2. Solving −0.41/√(1 − M²) = C_p,cr(M) by bisection: M_cr = 0.744.
  3. (11.5) p/p∞ = (p₀/p∞ at M = 0.8)/(p₀/p at M = 1) = 1.524/1.893 = 0.805.

Check: Solution Manual: the graph gives M_cr = 0.74 and p/p∞ = 0.805.

11.6 – 11.7. (11.6) An airfoil in an M∞ = 0.5 stream; at one point the local Mach number is 0.86. Find the pressure coefficient there. (11.7) Figure 11.5 of the book raises M∞ from 0.3 to M_cr = 0.61, with M_A = 0.435 at the minimum-pressure point A when M∞ = 0.3. Use Prandtl-Glauert to show that M_cr ≈ 0.61, i.e. M_A = 1 when M∞ = 0.61.

Anderson 6th, Problems 11.6, 11.7

Show the solution

What the problem says. Compressible flow: C_p is a function of two Mach numbers.

Idea. Use p/p∞ = (p₀/p∞)/(local p₀/p) then C_p = (2/γM∞²)(p/p∞ − 1).

Steps.

  1. (11.6) p₀/p∞ = 1.186; p₀/p (M = 0.86) = 1.621 ⇒ p/p∞ = 0.7319 ⇒ C_p = −1.53.
  2. (11.7) At M∞ = 0.3: C_p,A = −1.037 ⇒ C_p,0 = C_p,A√(1 − 0.09) = −0.989.
  3. At M∞ = 0.61: C_p,A = C_p,0/√(1 − 0.61²) = −1.249 ⇒ p/p∞ = 0.675.
  4. Solving M_A from p₀/p: M_A = 1.006 ≈ 1, so M_cr = 0.61.

Check: The Solution Manual interpolates tables so it has C_p,0 = −0.9969 (against −0.9894 here); the conclusion is the same.
