MODULE 08 · Lateral dynamics

Lateral dynamics: roll convergence, the spiral mode and the Dutch roll of the B747

Module 04 handled longitudinal stability and the two longitudinal modes. This module does the same for lateral motion: four variables [v, p, r, φ] and the B747 system matrix printed in Etkin, giving three modes: roll convergence (very fast), the spiral (very slow) and the Dutch roll (a lightly damped oscillation). We recompute the eigenvalues, the characteristic times of Table 6.8, the criteria E and R, and use a simulation demo to see the effect of a yaw damper.

Lateral stability derivatives

  • Three lateral quantities: side force Y, rolling moment l and yawing moment n
  • C_nβ positive: directional (weathercock) stability; C_lβ negative: a stabilising dihedral effect
  • C_lp negative and C_nr negative: roll damping and yaw damping

PART 2/5
02

The system matrix and the characteristic polynomial

  • State x = [v, p, r, φ], ẋ = A·x
  • A fourth-order polynomial: λ⁴ + Bλ³ + Cλ² + Dλ + E = 0
  • Routh stability condition: all coefficients positive and R = BCD − D² − B²E > 0

PART 3/5 · 08

The shape of each mode

  • Spiral: mainly yawing at nearly zero sideslip with some rolling, i.e. a truly banked turn (Etkin 6.7)
  • Roll convergence: the roll rate p dies out quickly after a rolling disturbance
  • Dutch roll: coupled yaw and roll with a period close to the longitudinal short period (6.6 s against 7.1 s)
📖 Explained for complete beginners
Etkin Exercise 6.11: in the Dutch roll the two wing tips move out of phase like an ice skater.
PART 4/5
04

The yaw damper and control

  • The Dutch roll is lightly damped on high-flying swept-wing aircraft
  • A yaw damper feeds yaw rate r to the rudder to add damping
  • Simple model: subtract ΔN_r from the r-r element of matrix A

📜 history

The name "Dutch roll"

Etkin (Exercise 6.11) explains that the name Dutch roll comes from the resemblance to the motion of a Dutch ice skater; he suggests holding a model aeroplane by its wing tips and practising the motion in front of a mirror. The mode became more important as swept-wing jets flew high, where natural damping is weak and the yaw damper became standard equipment.

🔎 case

Case: a 10 ft/s sideslip disturbance

For the B747 in Etkin, an initial 10 ft/s sideslip disturbance causes a yaw-roll oscillation of period 6.6 s whose amplitude only halves after 21 s (3.2 cycles). Adding ΔN_r = 0.5 to the model raises ζ to 0.29 and the oscillation almost vanishes within seconds (see the demo). This is why a yaw damper is essential for comfort and safety.

⚠️ watch out

Common pitfalls

(1) The spiral is stable (σ < 0) but very slow; the book only says there is no unstable mode. (2) Directional stability that is strong relative to lateral stability makes Dutch roll, too weak makes the spiral: the two demands conflict. (3) ΔN_r in the demo is a simplified model, not the rudder control loop. (4) Matrix A is for one flight condition (u₀ = 774 ft/s); change speed or altitude and the eigenvalues change. (5) EASA is qualitative; do not use its figures to compute the Dutch roll.
