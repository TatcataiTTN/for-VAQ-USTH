Lab: calculators to check your hand work

Five groups of calculators run entirely in your browser (nothing is sent anywhere): Otto cycle, Diesel cycle, Brayton cycle, isentropic flow and nozzles, combustion balance. Each shows its steps with formulas. "Load worked example" fills in a problem with a printed or twice-recomputed answer, then compares with the reference values and names the source. Inputs accept a decimal comma or a decimal point (1,4 or 1.4).

Scope and assumptions
  • Ideal gas with a constant specific-heat ratio k (air-standard / cold-air). Defaults for air: k = 1.4; R = 0.287 kJ/(kg·K) (Appendix 1 Table A-2 (a)); Mair = 28.97 kg/kmol (Table A-1).
  • The calculators check a hand calculation, they do not replace it. Problems that use ideal-gas tables (variable cp) give different numbers from a constant k: Tutorials I problem 1 gives η = 0.523 with tables but 0.565 as cold-air.
  • SI units: K, kPa, kJ/kg, kJ/(kg·K), m³/kg, kg/s. Convert °C to K (+273 or +273.15) before typing.

NOT DONE in this round

  • R-134a vapour-compression cycle (refrigeration): needs the property library (P3), which does not exist yet.
  • Every problem that uses ideal-gas tables with variable cp (Appendix 1 Table A-17, A-18…): needs the property library (P3), not available yet.
  • Dew point of the combustion products: needs Psat(T) of water from the tables. NOT DONE (needs the property library P3); no Antoine formula is used instead.
  • Incomplete combustion (CO), rich mixtures, heat of reaction and flame temperature: outside this version.
  • Normal and oblique shocks, Fanno/Rayleigh flow and converging–diverging nozzles with shocks: outside this version (the checked Chapter 5 examples do not need them).

1. Ideal Otto cycle

Isentropic compression, constant-volume heat addition, isentropic expansion, constant-volume heat rejection. Give r, k, T₁, P₁ and one of q_in, T₃, T₄.

2. Ideal Diesel cycle

Constant-pressure heat addition. Give r, k, T₁, P₁ and one of r_c, T₃, q_in. The η formula in r, r_c, k is shown too.

3. Brayton cycle

Optional isentropic efficiencies of compressor and turbine, regeneration (effectiveness ε), intercooling and reheating (number of stages, equal pressure ratio per stage, slide Ch.2 p.33–48). Shows the pressure ratio that maximises w_net.

4. Isentropic flow and nozzles (Chapter 5)

Six small tools: (a) from Ma, (b) from A/A* (both branches), (c) from P/P₀, (d) stagnation properties, (e) critical ratios and choked flow, (f) converging nozzle with back pressure. k and R are editable; defaults are for air.

(a) From the Mach number: T/T₀, P/P₀, ρ/ρ₀, A/A*

(b) From A/A*: subsonic and supersonic Mach numbers; duct with an area change

(c) From P/P₀: Mach number (and T, c, V, area when T₀, P₀, ṁ are known)

(d) Stagnation properties T₀, P₀ from T, P, V

(e) Critical ratios and choked flow at the throat

(f) Converging nozzle with back pressure: choked or not

5. Combustion balance (Chapter 4)

Any fuel CaHbOcNd burning completely in air (3.76 N₂ per O₂), stoichiometric or lean. Gives the balance coefficients, AF on a mass and a mole basis, and the product composition.

NOT DONE Dew point: needs the property library P3 (Psat(T) of water). The calculator only gives the partial pressure of the water vapour Pv = yH₂O·P.

Source of the molar masses. C = 12.011 (Appendix 1 Table A-27, Carbon row); H = 1.008; O = 15.999; N = 14.007. The Appendix lists no atomic masses: the last three are half the molecular masses of Table A-1 (H₂ 2.016; O₂ 31.999; N₂ 28.013) rounded to 0.001. Computed molar masses therefore differ from the tables by up to 0.001 kg/kmol (C₈H₁₈: 114.232 vs 114.231 in Table A-27). Mair = 28.97 (Table A-1).
