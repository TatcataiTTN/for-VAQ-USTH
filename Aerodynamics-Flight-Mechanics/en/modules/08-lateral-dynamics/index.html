MODULE 08 · Lateral dynamics

Lateral dynamics: roll convergence, the spiral mode and the Dutch roll of the B747

Module 04 handled longitudinal stability and the two longitudinal modes. This module does the same for lateral motion. There are four variables [v, p, r, φ] and the B747 system matrix printed in Etkin, giving three modes: roll convergence (very fast), the spiral (very slow) and the Dutch roll (a lightly damped oscillation). We recompute the eigenvalues, the characteristic times of Table 6.8 and the criteria E and R, then use a simulation demo to see what a yaw damper does.

01

Lateral stability derivatives

  • Three lateral quantities: side force Y, rolling moment l and yawing moment n
  • C_nβ positive: directional (weathercock) stability; C_lβ negative: a stabilising dihedral effect
  • C_lp negative and C_nr negative: roll damping and yaw damping

PART 2/5
02

The system matrix and the characteristic polynomial

  • State x = [v, p, r, φ], ẋ = A·x
  • A fourth-order polynomial: λ⁴ + Bλ³ + Cλ² + Dλ + E = 0
  • Routh stability condition: all coefficients positive and R = BCD − D² − B²E > 0

PART 3/5 · 08

The shape of each mode

  • Spiral: mainly yawing at nearly zero sideslip with some rolling, i.e. a truly banked turn (Etkin 6.7)
  • Roll convergence: the roll rate p dies out quickly after a rolling disturbance
  • Dutch roll: coupled yaw and roll with a period close to the longitudinal short period (6.6 s against 7.1 s)
In plain words
Etkin Exercise 6.11: in the Dutch roll the two wing tips move out of phase like an ice skater.
PART 4/5
04

The yaw damper and control

  • The Dutch roll is lightly damped on high-flying swept-wing aircraft
  • A yaw damper feeds yaw rate r to the rudder to add damping
  • Simple model: subtract ΔN_r from the r-r element of matrix A

History

The name "Dutch roll"

In Exercise 6.11 Etkin explains that the name Dutch roll comes from the resemblance to the motion of a Dutch ice skater. He suggests holding a model aeroplane by its wing tips and trying the motion in front of a mirror. The mode became more important as swept-wing jets flew high, where natural damping is weak and the yaw damper became standard equipment.

Case

Case: a 10 ft/s sideslip disturbance

  • For the B747 in Etkin, an initial 10 ft/s sideslip disturbance causes a yaw-roll oscillation of period 6.6 s. Its amplitude only halves after 21 s (3.2 cycles).
  • Adding ΔN_r = 0.5 to the model raises ζ to 0.29 and the oscillation almost vanishes within seconds (see the demo).
  • That is why a yaw damper is essential for both comfort and safety.
Pitfalls

Common pitfalls

  • The spiral of the B747 in this example is stable (σ < 0) but very slow. Etkin also says the spiral is often unstable over part of the speed and altitude range; its time scales are so long that handling qualities are not much affected.
  • Strong directional stability relative to the dihedral effect makes the spiral prone to instability (E > 0 needs C_lβ·C_nr > C_lr·C_nβ, Etkin 6.8). Conversely, weak directional stability relative to dihedral makes the Dutch roll lightly damped. The two demands conflict, so the design has to pick a midpoint.
  • ΔN_r in the demo is a simplified model, not the real rudder control loop.
  • Matrix A is for one flight condition (u₀ = 774 ft/s); change speed or altitude and the eigenvalues change.
  • EASA is only qualitative; do not use its figures to compute the Dutch roll.

Two problems solved with Etkin's B747 data. The answers match the numbers printed in the book (Table 6.8, equation 6.7,2).

6.7. From the B747 lateral matrix A (Etkin 6.7,1) find the three lateral modes and their characteristic times (Table 6.8).

Etkin & Reid 3rd, sec. 6.7, Table 6.8

Show the solution

What the problem says. Given the lateral system matrix A of the B747 (state [v, p, r, φ], Etkin 6.7,1), find the three lateral modes and their characteristic times (Table 6.8).

Idea. The modes are the roots of the characteristic polynomial, i.e. the eigenvalues of A. Real roots give non-oscillatory modes (roll convergence, spiral) and the complex pair gives the Dutch roll. The characteristic times follow from t½ = ln 2/|σ|, T = 2π/ω and N½ = t½/T.

Steps.

  1. Eigenvalues of A: spiral λ = −0.00730; roll convergence λ = −0.56248; Dutch roll λ = −0.03301 ± 0.94655i.
  2. For the two real modes t½ = 0.6931/|λ|, so the spiral has t½ = 0.6931/0.00730 = 95.0 s and roll convergence has t½ = 0.6931/0.56248 = 1.23 s.
  3. Dutch roll: T = 2π/0.94655 = 6.64 s; t½ = 0.6931/0.03301 = 21.0 s; N½ = t½/T = 3.16 cycles.
  4. Damping ratio: ζ = −σ/|λ| = 0.03301/0.9471 = 0.035.

Check: This matches Etkin's Table 6.8 (95 s; 1.23 s; T = 6.64 s; t½ = 21 s; N½ = 3.16). All three modes have negative real parts so they are stable, but the spiral is very slow and the Dutch roll is lightly damped (ζ ≈ 0.035).

6.4. Check for the lateral motion of the B747 that the λ³ coefficient of the characteristic polynomial equals −ΣRe λ.

Etkin & Reid 3rd, Exercise 6.4

Show the solution

What the problem says. Check for the lateral motion of the B747 that the λ³ coefficient of the characteristic polynomial equals −ΣRe λ.

Idea. As in module 04: the coefficient of λ^(N−1) is −Σλ_i, and complex conjugate roots have imaginary parts that sum to zero. Here there are two real roots and one complex pair, so −Σλ_i = −(2 Re λ_DR + λ_roll + λ_spiral).

Steps.

  1. −ΣRe λ = 2 × 0.03301 + 0.56248 + 0.007297 = 0.63580.
  2. The coefficient printed in (6.7,2) is B = 0.6358: it matches.
  3. The remaining two criteria: E = 0.003682 > 0 and R = BCD − D² − B²E = 0.04223 > 0.

Check: Since all coefficients are positive and E > 0, R > 0, the Routh criteria say all three modes are stable, consistent with the three eigenvalues having negative real parts in 6.7. R differs slightly from the printed 0.04223 because the book rounds the coefficients before multiplying.
